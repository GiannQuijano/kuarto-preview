Why most pool projects stall

You wanted a pool. You got a nine-month dirt pit.

The typical pool build in Southern California runs six months to a year. The delays almost always come from the same three places.

Too many hands

A pool company digs, a mason sets stone, a landscaper finishes, and nobody owns the schedule. Every hand-off adds weeks.

Catalog design

Kidney shapes, blue plaster and stamped concrete that fight the architecture of a modern home instead of finishing it.

A build with no end date

Open-ended timelines mean a torn-up yard through the summer you were building it for.

The 90-day build

A fixed sequence, run by one builder.

Your design is finalized and permits are approved before we break ground. From there, every trade works off one schedule that we own.

Days 1–7

Layout & excavation

Site protection, layout to plan, dig and haul-off.

Days 8–20

Steel, plumbing, electrical

Rebar cage, plumbing runs, bonding and inspection.

Days 21–30

Shell

Shotcrete shell, spa and benches, then a controlled cure.

Days 31–55

Tile, coping, equipment

Waterline tile, stone coping, pumps, heater and automation.

Days 56–80

Hardscape & lighting

Decking, steps, planters, fire features and landscape lighting.

Days 81–90

Finish & first swim

Interior finish, fill, start-up, final inspection and walkthrough.

Design, engineering and permitting take place before day 1 and usually run 4–8 weeks depending on your city. Weather days and owner-requested changes can extend the schedule; we put the build calendar in writing.

Ninety days runs from ground break to a finished, filled pool with hardscape complete. Design and permitting happen first and usually take 4 to 8 weeks depending on your city. Your build calendar is written into the contract.

Yes. We prepare engineering, pull permits, schedule every inspection and put together the drawings your HOA needs.
